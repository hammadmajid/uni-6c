<Predict
  id="aq-long-classify-taxi"
  prompt="Classify the automated-taxi environment on all six of lecture 3's axes, one line of justification each. (6 marks)"
  answer="Partially observable — sensors cannot see around trucks or behind the car. Stochastic — traffic, weather and other drivers make outcomes uncertain. Sequential — where the taxi is depends on every earlier action. Dynamic — the world keeps moving while the agent decides. Continuous — speed, steering and position vary smoothly. Known — the rules of the road and of physics are given (it is the state that is hidden, not the rules). It sits at almost every hard pole, which is why it needs model-based, utility-based and learning designs together."
  placeholder="Observable: … Deterministic: … Episodic: … Static: … Discrete: … Known: …"
>

- **Partially observable:** sensors miss what is behind a truck or around a corner.
- **Stochastic:** other drivers, pedestrians and weather make the outcome of an action uncertain.
- **Sequential:** the current position and speed depend on every earlier decision.
- **Dynamic:** the environment changes while the agent is deciding, so a stale decision is dangerous.
- **Continuous:** speed, steering angle and position are real-valued, and time flows continuously.
- **Known:** the rules of the road and of physics are known in advance — do not confuse this with observability; the rules are known even though the state is not fully visible.

Closer for full marks: because it lands on so many hard poles at once, the taxi needs the most capable agents from lecture 2 — memory (model-based), trade-offs (utility-based) and improvement (learning).

</Predict>
