The usual wrong answers are the three traps this lab keeps returning to. **COMPANY** owns the database and is not stored in it. **COMMISSION** is a value recorded per sale, not a thing with its own identity. **"1, 2, 3, 4, 5 years"** are five rows of REPLACEMENT_SCHEME, not five entities.

## The case, as the slide gives it

> A car sales company offers to its customers the option of purchasing a new car using replacement scheme of over 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, 4 years and 5 years. A customer may choose one of the replacement scheme options for each purchase or may pay the whole amount in one go, meaning a replacement scheme option may be selected by one or more customers or may not be selected by at all. A customer will have a unique customer number, a name and address and a phone number. A replacement scheme will have a unique replacement scheme number, a name and the number of years for payment. A car will have a unique car code, a make a model, year of manufacturing and a price.
>
> The company pays commission on sale of cars to its sales people and therefore it will also keep information about each sale which includes a unique sale id and commission to be paid on that sale. The company will also want to know who made the sale and the car involved in that transaction so that the commission can be determined. A sale person will have a unique salesperson id, a name, a name a phone number and email address.

The last sentence says "a name, a name a phone number": a typo on the slide. The salesperson has **one** Name attribute. If you copy the case onto your answer sheet, do not draw two.

## The five-step procedure

Every ERD question can be solved with the same five passes over the text. Doing them in order stops you from drawing a diamond before you know what it connects.

1. **Nouns.** Underline every noun. Each one is an entity, an attribute, a synonym of something you already have, a value (a row), or not stored at all.
2. **Keys.** For each entity, find the attribute the text calls unique. It is underlined in Chen notation.
3. **Verbs.** Each verb that links two entities ("made the sale", "the car involved") becomes a relationship diamond.
4. **Numbers.** For each relationship, read the wording for how many (1 or N on each side) and whether participation is compulsory (a double line) or optional (a single line).
5. **Draw.** Entities first, then attributes, then diamonds and lines, then the 1 and N labels last.

Two tags need a word more than the feedback gives.

**Synonyms.** The case says *purchase*, *sale* and *transaction*. It is one event seen from the customer's side, from the company's side, and from an accountant's. Name the entity after the noun the text gives an identifier to (a unique **sale** id), so it is SALE.

**Values.** "1 year, 2 years, … 5 years" is not a design decision; it is data. Those five options are five rows of REPLACEMENT_SCHEME with NoOfYears = 1 to 5. If you draw them as five attributes or five entities, adding a 6-year plan would mean changing the schema instead of inserting a row.

## Step 3: verbs become relationships

Read the text again for verbs that link two of your five entities:

| Wording in the case | Relationship |
|---|---|
| a customer … **for each purchase** | CUSTOMER **BUYS** SALE |
| **who made the sale** | SALESPERSON **MAKES** SALE |
| **the car involved** in that transaction | SALE **FOR** CAR |
| **may choose** one of the replacement scheme options **for each purchase** | SALE **PAID_UNDER** REPLACEMENT_SCHEME |

Notice that all four touch SALE. When an event (a sale, an order, an enrolment) has its own identifier, it becomes the hub, and the things it involves hang off it.

## Step 4: cardinality and participation from the wording

Cardinality asks "how many at most": one sale has **one** customer, and a customer can have **many** sales, so BUYS is 1:N. Participation asks "must every instance take part?": every sale must have a customer (total, drawn as a double line), but a customer does not have to be in the database through a purchase (partial, a single line).

| Relationship | Ratio | Participation | The words that decide it |
|---|---|---|---|
| CUSTOMER BUYS SALE | 1:N | SALE total, CUSTOMER partial | each purchase is by one customer; a customer may make many |
| SALESPERSON MAKES SALE | 1:N | SALE total, SALESPERSON partial | "who made the sale": one person per sale, needed for commission |
| SALE FOR CAR | 1:1 | SALE total, CAR partial | a unique car code identifies one physical car, sold once; unsold cars are in stock |
| SALE PAID_UNDER REPLACEMENT_SCHEME | N:1 | both partial | "may pay the whole amount in one go"; a scheme "may not be selected at all" |

Two of those rows deserve an honest note.

- **CUSTOMER partial or total?** Partial is the safer reading: a customer record can exist before its first purchase. If you argue that the company only records someone once they buy, total is defensible. Either way, **write your assumption next to the diagram**. Markers reward a stated assumption far more often than they punish it.
- **SALE FOR CAR, 1:1 or N:1?** 1:1 assumes a car code names one physical car, which a unique code, a year and a price suggest. If you read CAR as a catalogue model ("Corolla 2025") that is sold many times, it is N:1. State which you mean.

<Deeper kind="exam" title="Model answer in written form (7 marks)">

**Entities and attributes** (keys underlined on paper, marked "key" here):

- CUSTOMER (CustomerNo key, Name, Address, Phone)
- REPLACEMENT_SCHEME (SchemeNo key, Name, NoOfYears)
- CAR (CarCode key, Make, Model, YearOfManufacture, Price)
- SALE (SaleId key, Commission)
- SALESPERSON (SalespersonId key, Name, Phone, Email)

**Relationships:**

- CUSTOMER BUYS SALE, 1:N. Every sale has exactly one customer (total on SALE); a customer may have many sales.
- SALESPERSON MAKES SALE, 1:N. Every sale is made by one salesperson (total on SALE), which is how commission is determined.
- SALE FOR CAR, 1:1. Every sale is for one car (total on SALE); a car is sold at most once, and unsold cars exist (partial on CAR). Assumption: a car code identifies one physical car.
- SALE PAID_UNDER REPLACEMENT_SCHEME, N:1. Partial on both sides: a sale may be paid in one go, and a scheme may never be chosen.

**Assumptions stated:** customer participation in BUYS is partial (a customer can be registered before a purchase); the five payment periods are rows of REPLACEMENT_SCHEME.

</Deeper>

<Deeper kind="beyond" title="The same design as tables (the lecture's outcome 6, next lecture)">

Lecture 3's learning outcomes end with "map an ER model to a relational schema". The rule for a 1:N relationship is: put the key of the 1 side into the table of the N side as a foreign key. Every relationship here has SALE on its N (or total) side, so every foreign key lands in SALE:

```sql
CREATE TABLE sale (
  sale_id        integer PRIMARY KEY,
  commission     numeric(10,2) NOT NULL,
  customer_no    integer NOT NULL REFERENCES customer,          -- BUYS, total
  salesperson_id integer NOT NULL REFERENCES salesperson,       -- MAKES, total
  car_code       text    NOT NULL UNIQUE REFERENCES car,        -- FOR, 1:1
  scheme_no      integer          REFERENCES replacement_scheme -- PAID_UNDER, partial
);
```

Read the constraints back against the diagram. **NOT NULL** is total participation. **UNIQUE** on car_code is the "1" on SALE's side of FOR: no car can appear in two sales. The one nullable column, scheme_no, is the single line for "paid in one go". The diagram and the DDL are the same decisions written twice.

</Deeper>
