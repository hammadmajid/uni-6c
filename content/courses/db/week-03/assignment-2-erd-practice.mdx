<Predict
  id="a2-case1"
  prompt="A university keeps students, each with a unique registration number, a name made of first and last name, a date of birth, an age, and one or more phone numbers. A course has a unique course code, a title and credit hours. A teacher has a unique teacher ID, a name and a designation. A department has a unique department code and a name. A student enrols in many courses and a course has many students; for each enrolment the semester and the grade are recorded.   answer="Four entities: STUDENT, COURSE, TEACHER, DEPARTMENT. ENROLS M:N with Semester and Grade on the diamond, both partial. TEACHES 1:N, COURSE total. BELONGS_TO N:1, both total. HEADS 1:1, DEPARTMENT total, TEACHER partial."
>

**Model answer (Chen).**

| Entity | Attributes |
|---|---|
| STUDENT | **RegNo** (key, underlined), Name (composite: FirstName, LastName as sub-ovals), DOB, Age (derived, dashed oval), Phone (multivalued, double oval) |
| COURSE | **CourseCode** (key), Title, CreditHours |
| TEACHER | **TeacherID** (key), Name, Designation |
| DEPARTMENT | **DeptCode** (key), Name |

| Relationship | Between | Ratio | Participation | The words that decide it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ENROLS | STUDENT and COURSE | M:N | both partial | "many courses", "many students"; "may be enrolled in no course", "no students yet" |
| TEACHES | TEACHER and COURSE | 1:N (1 by TEACHER) | COURSE total, TEACHER partial | "exactly one teacher"; "several courses or none" |
| BELONGS_TO | TEACHER and DEPARTMENT | N:1 (1 by DEPARTMENT) | both total | "every teacher … exactly one department"; "at least one teacher" |
| HEADS | TEACHER and DEPARTMENT | 1:1 | DEPARTMENT total, TEACHER partial | "every department … exactly one teacher"; "at most one department" |

Semester and Grade are ovals attached to the ENROLS diamond.

**Mark yourself, one mark each:** four entities and no extras (no SEMESTER, no GRADE, no UNIVERSITY); every key underlined; Name composite, Age dashed, Phone double oval; Semester and Grade on the diamond; four relationships including two separate diamonds between TEACHER and DEPARTMENT; correct 1, N and M labels; the four double lines.

**Layout that avoids crossing lines:** STUDENT, COURSE, TEACHER, DEPARTMENT left to right in one row, with ENROLS and TEACHES between neighbours, BELONGS_TO above the last pair and HEADS below it.

</Predict>

## Case 2: food delivery (20 minutes)

<HintLadder
  id="a2-case2-hints"
  hints={[
    "Five nouns get a unique identifier. A sixth thing, the order line, is only numbered within its order.",
    "ORDER_LINE is a weak entity owned by ORDER. Its line number is a partial key. Quantity sits on ORDER_LINE.",
    "ORDER is the hub: it touches CUSTOMER, RIDER and ORDER_LINE. MENU_ITEM connects to ORDER_LINE and to RESTAURANT, not to ORDER directly.",
    "Total is calculated, so it is derived. The delivery address is composite. 'Until one is assigned' makes ORDER partial in the rider relationship."
  ]}
/>

<Predict
  id="a2-case2"
  prompt="A food delivery company registers customers, each with a unique customer ID, a name, a phone number and a delivery address made of house, street and city. A restaurant has a unique restaurant ID, a name and a city. Each restaurant offers many menu items; a menu item has a unique item code, a name and a price, and belongs to exactly one restaurant. A customer places orders; an order has a unique order number, an order date, a status and a total that is calculated from its lines. Every order is placed by exactly one customer, and a customer may not have ordered yet. An order contains one or more order lines; lines are numbered 1, 2, 3 within each order, and each line records a quantity and refers to exactly one menu item. A menu item may appear on many order lines or on none. Riders have a unique rider ID, a name and a phone number. An order is delivered by at most one rider and has no rider until one is assigned; a rider delivers many orders. Draw the complete Chen ER diagram on paper, then write the entity and relationship lists."
  answer="CUSTOMER, RESTAURANT, MENU_ITEM, ORDER, RIDER strong; ORDER_LINE weak under ORDER (LineNo partial key, Quantity). PLACES 1:N, ORDER total. CONTAINS identifying 1:N, both total. REFERS_TO N:1, ORDER_LINE total. OFFERS 1:N, MENU_ITEM total. DELIVERS 1:N, both partial."
>

**Model answer (Chen).**

| Entity | Attributes |
|---|---|
| CUSTOMER | **CustomerID** (key), Name, Phone, Address (composite: House, Street, City) |
| RESTAURANT | **RestaurantID** (key), Name, City |
| MENU_ITEM | **ItemCode** (key), Name, Price |
| ORDER | **OrderNo** (key), OrderDate, Status, Total (derived, dashed oval) |
| ORDER_LINE (weak, double rectangle) | LineNo (partial key, dashed underline), Quantity |
| RIDER | **RiderID** (key), Name, Phone |

| Relationship | Between | Ratio | Participation | The words that decide it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PLACES | CUSTOMER and ORDER | 1:N (1 by CUSTOMER) | ORDER total, CUSTOMER partial | "exactly one customer"; "may not have ordered yet" |
| CONTAINS (identifying, double diamond) | ORDER and ORDER_LINE | 1:N (1 by ORDER) | both total | "one or more order lines"; a weak entity is always total |
| REFERS_TO | ORDER_LINE and MENU_ITEM | N:1 (1 by MENU_ITEM) | ORDER_LINE total, MENU_ITEM partial | "exactly one menu item"; "or on none" |
| OFFERS | RESTAURANT and MENU_ITEM | 1:N (1 by RESTAURANT) | MENU_ITEM total, RESTAURANT partial | "belongs to exactly one restaurant" |
| DELIVERS | RIDER and ORDER | 1:N (1 by RIDER) | both partial | "at most one rider", "no rider until one is assigned" |

**Assumptions to write beside the diagram:** a restaurant can be registered before it lists any item (RESTAURANT partial in OFFERS); a rider may not have delivered anything yet; Phone is single-valued because the text says "a phone number".

**Also acceptable:** no ORDER_LINE entity, and instead an M:N relationship CONTAINS between ORDER and MENU_ITEM with Quantity on the diamond. It stores the same facts as long as an item appears once per order. Because the text numbers the lines, the weak entity is the closer reading; if you choose the M:N version, say why in one line.

**Mark yourself, one mark each:** six entities with the weak one in a double rectangle; keys underlined and LineNo dashed; Address composite and Total dashed; five relationships with CONTAINS as a double diamond; Quantity on ORDER_LINE; correct 1 and N labels; double lines on ORDER into PLACES, both sides of CONTAINS, ORDER_LINE into REFERS_TO and MENU_ITEM into OFFERS.

</Predict>

## On the day

<Predict
  id="a2-checklist"
  prompt="From memory, write the checks you will run over your finished diagram in the last two minutes. Aim for at least six."
  answer="Every entity has an underlined key; every relationship has a label on both ends; participation shown; special attributes drawn with the right oval; no value or owner drawn as an entity; assumptions written; name and registration number on every page."
>

**The last-two-minutes checklist.**

| Check | What a miss looks like |
|---|---|
| Every rectangle has exactly one underlined key (dashed for a weak entity) | An entity with ovals but nothing underlined |
| Every diamond has a 1, N or M on both of its lines | A bare line, which the marker cannot read as any ratio |
| Double lines where the text says "every", "must" or "exactly one" | All single lines, so participation was skipped |
| Composite, multivalued and derived attributes use their own shapes | "One or more phone numbers" drawn as a plain oval |
| Nothing drawn for the organisation that owns the database, or for values | A COMPANY rectangle, or one entity per option |
| Attributes that depend on a pair sit on the diamond | Grade on STUDENT, quantity on PRODUCT |
| Assumptions written in one or two lines under the diagram | An ambiguous sentence resolved silently |
| Name and registration number on every loose page, behind the front page | Unstapled white pages with no name |

Draw in pen, large, with the hub entity in the middle of the page. If a first attempt gets tangled, redraw it on a fresh page; that is what the spare white pages are for.

</Predict>
