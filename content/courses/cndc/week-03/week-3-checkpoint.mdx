## Long answers

<Predict
  id="cp3-lan-man-wan"
  prompt="Differentiate between LAN, MAN and WAN. (5 marks)"
  answer="Definitions with examples (LAN: lab or office; MAN: city cable TV or metro network; WAN: nationwide or the Internet), then a table on ownership, area, design and maintenance, medium, data rate and delay."
>

A **LAN** covers a small area such as a building or campus, is privately owned, and is easy to design and maintain. Example: a university lab. A **MAN** spans a city, can be private or public, and uses LAN-like technology over fibre or coax. Example: a city's cable TV network. A **WAN** spans countries using leased lines, PSTN or satellite links. Example: a bank's national branch network, or the Internet.

| Parameter | LAN | MAN | WAN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Private | Private or public | Private or public |
| Area | Small | Moderate (city) | Very large |
| Design and maintenance | Easy | Not easy | Not easy |
| Medium | Twisted pair, coax, Wi-Fi | Fibre, coax, wireless | Leased lines, PSTN, satellite |
| Data rate | High | Moderate | Low per connection |
| Delay and errors | Low | Moderate | High |

<Predict
  id="cp3-topologies"
  prompt="Explain bus, star and ring topologies with one advantage and one disadvantage of each. (6 marks)"
  answer="Bus: one trunk that all nodes tap onto; cheap and simple; one trunk break kills the whole network. Star: every node cabled to a central hub or switch; a faulty cable isolates only its own node; the hub is a single point of failure. Ring: each node connects to the next and repeats the signal, with a token deciding who sends; no collisions; one failed node or cable stops a simple ring."
>

**Bus**: all computers attach to a single cable (trunk or backbone), terminated at both ends; only one computer can send at a time. *Advantage*: simple, lowest cost, least cable. *Disadvantage*: a break in the trunk stops the whole network, and faults are hard to find.

**Star**: each computer has its own cable to a central hub or switch. *Advantage*: a cable fault affects only its computer, and nodes are easy to add. *Disadvantage*: more cable, and if the hub fails the entire network is down.

**Ring**: each computer connects to two neighbours in a closed loop and acts as a repeater; a token circulates, and only its holder may transmit. *Advantage*: no collisions, and the signal is regenerated at every node. *Disadvantage*: hard to add computers, and one failed computer or link breaks the ring (unless a dual ring is used).

Draw a small sketch of each; sketches are marked.

</Predict>

<Predict
  id="cp3-impairment-long"
  prompt="What is transmission impairment? Explain its three causes. (5 marks)"
  answer="The received signal differs from the transmitted one. Attenuation: energy lost with distance, measured in dB, fixed by amplifiers or repeaters. Distortion: change of shape because frequency components travel at different speeds (delay distortion). Noise: unwanted signals added in transit, such as thermal, induced, crosstalk and impulse noise."
>

**Transmission impairment**: the signal at the receiver is not identical to the signal sent, because the medium is imperfect.

1. **Attenuation**: the signal loses energy as it travels, so its strength falls with distance. It is measured in decibels, dB = 10 log₁₀(P₂ ÷ P₁); halving the power is −3 dB. Amplifiers or repeaters restore it.
2. **Distortion**: the signal changes shape. A composite signal's frequency components travel at different speeds, arrive at different times, and no longer line up (delay distortion).
3. **Noise**: unwanted energy added to the signal: thermal (random electron motion), induced (motors, appliances), crosstalk (from a neighbouring wire), impulse (lightning, power lines). It is measured against the signal as the SNR.

</Predict>

<Predict
  id="cp3-lpwan-long"
  prompt="What is LPWAN and why is it needed for IoT? (4 marks)"
  answer="Low Power Wide Area Network. IoT devices need long range, low data rate and years of battery life. Existing technologies are either short range (Bluetooth, ZigBee, Wi-Fi) or power-hungry (cellular). LPWAN fills the long-range, low-bandwidth gap. Examples: LoRaWAN, Sigfox, NB-IoT."
>

**LPWAN** (Low Power Wide Area Network) is a class of wireless technologies for long-range, low-bandwidth, low-power communication, aimed at IoT and M2M.

IoT devices such as meters, sensors and trackers send small amounts of data, are often far from infrastructure, and run on batteries for years. Short-range technologies (Bluetooth, ZigBee, Wi-Fi) do not reach far enough; cellular 3G and 4G reach, but draw too much power and cost too much per device. On the range and bandwidth map, the long-range, low-bandwidth corner was empty. LPWAN fills it, trading data rate for kilometres of range and multi-year battery life. Examples: **LoRaWAN** and **Sigfox** (unlicensed spectrum), **NB-IoT** and **LTE-M** (licensed, run by mobile operators).

</Predict>
