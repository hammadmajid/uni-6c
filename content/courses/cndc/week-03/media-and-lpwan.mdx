**3 dB.** Attenuation in decibels is 10 log₁₀(P_out ÷ P_in) = 10 log₁₀(0.5) ≈ **−3 dB**. The minus sign means loss. Halving the power is always −3 dB, whatever the absolute power.

**No, not as good as new.** The cable added noise along the way, and an amplifier cannot tell noise from signal. It scales both, so the picture gets bigger but the **signal-to-noise ratio** does not improve. The fix for digital signals is a **repeater**, which decides each bit and sends a fresh clean copy. ## Guided and unguided

<MediaTree />

**Guided** media carry the signal inside a physical path: copper wire or glass. **Unguided** media broadcast it through air, water or space, so anyone with an antenna in range can receive it. The lecture concentrates on guided media.

<MediaCutaway />

- **Twisted pair.** Two insulated copper wires twisted together. Interference hits both wires of a pair almost equally, and the receiver reads the *difference* between them, so the noise cancels. The tighter the twist, the better (Cat 6 twists more than Cat 5e). **UTP** is unshielded: cheap, flexible, and in nearly every LAN. **STP** adds a foil or braided shield: better against electromagnetic interference (EMI), but stiffer, dearer, and it has to be grounded.
- **Coaxial.** A central copper core, an insulator, a braided metal shield and a jacket. The shield keeps EMI out, so coax carries more bandwidth further than twisted pair. **Baseband** coax carries one digital signal (old 10BASE2 Ethernet). **Broadband** coax carries many FDM channels (cable TV and HFC Internet).
- **Fibre optic.** Light in a glass core, held there by **total internal reflection** at the boundary with the cladding. It is immune to EMI, hard to tap, and loses only about 0.2 dB per km, so spans reach tens of kilometres. **Multimode** fibre has a thick core (50 or 62.5 µm): cheap optics, but light takes paths of different lengths, which smears pulses, so it is limited to a few hundred metres inside buildings. **Single-mode** fibre has a 9 µm core and a laser: one path, tens of kilometres, the backbone.

## What happens on the way: impairments

The lecture names three factors to weigh when choosing a medium. **Bandwidth**: the wider it is, the higher the data rate it can carry. **Transmission impairment**: the received signal is not identical to the one sent. **Interference**: unwanted signals added from outside. Impairment has three causes: **attenuation**, **distortion** and **noise**. Watch all three before reading the definitions:

<ImpairmentLab />

- **Attenuation**: the signal loses energy with distance, heating the wire or scattering in the glass. It is measured in decibels, and amplifiers (analog) or repeaters (digital) compensate for it.
- **Distortion**: the signal changes shape. A signal is made of many frequency components, and each travels at a slightly different speed, so they arrive at different times and the edges smear. This is **delay distortion**. In multimode fibre the same effect comes from different light paths.
- **Noise**: unwanted energy added on the way. Types: **thermal** (random electron motion, always present), **induced** (motors and appliances), **crosstalk** (one wire picking up its neighbour, which is what twisting fights), and **impulse** (spikes from lightning and power lines).

<Callout type="key-idea" title="Decibels add">
Because a decibel is a logarithm, gains and losses along a path simply **add**. dB = 10 log₁₀(P₂ ÷ P₁). Halving the power is −3 dB, a tenth is −10 dB, a hundredth is −20 dB. A 3 dB loss, then a 7 dB amplifier, then another 3 dB loss comes to −3 + 7 − 3 = **+1 dB** overall.
</Callout>

<Deeper kind="formal" title="Signal-to-noise ratio and why it caps the data rate">

SNR = average signal power ÷ average noise power, usually quoted as SNR_dB = 10 log₁₀ SNR. Shannon's capacity theorem says the most a channel of bandwidth B can carry is **C = B log₂(1 + SNR)** bits per second, however clever the encoding. A 3 kHz telephone channel with 30 dB SNR (SNR = 1000) tops out near 3000 × log₂(1001) ≈ 30 kbps, which is why dial-up modems stalled at 33.6 kbps. It also explains the lab: an amplifier multiplies signal and noise by the same factor, so SNR, and with it the capacity, is unchanged.

</Deeper>

## IoT and LPWAN

The **Internet of Things** is the network of physical objects (sensors, meters, vehicles, buildings) with embedded electronics and connectivity, so they can collect and exchange data and be sensed and controlled remotely. The lecture's "Internet of Everything" picture adds people, process and data, and names the links: people to people (P2P), people to machine (P2M), and **machine to machine (M2M)**.

Most IoT devices want an odd combination: **long range, a tiny data rate, and a battery that lasts years**. A water meter sends a few bytes a day, from a basement, 5 km from the nearest gateway. Map existing wireless technologies by range and rate and that corner is empty:

<RangeBandwidthMap />

**LPWAN** (low-power wide-area network) fills it. It trades data rate for range and battery life: kilometres of range, tens of bits to tens of kilobits per second, years on a coin cell.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Who runs it | Anyone: buy a gateway | Sigfox operator | Mobile operator | Mobile operator |

<Deeper kind="beyond" title="Why LoRa reaches 15 km on milliwatts">

LoRa uses **chirp spread spectrum**: each symbol is a frequency sweep across the channel, and a receiver can pull a chirp out from *below* the noise floor, at roughly −20 dB SNR. The cost is time. At the slowest setting a 12-byte message takes over a second on air, and regional duty-cycle rules (1% in Europe's 868 MHz band) cap each device at a few such messages a minute. Range, rate and airtime trade against each other directly: this is Shannon's C = B log₂(1 + SNR) at the very low-SNR end.

</Deeper>

<Deeper kind="exam" title="Model answer: explain the causes of transmission impairment (5 marks)">

Define impairment in one line: the received signal differs from the transmitted one. Then three causes, each with a definition, a cause and a remedy. **Attenuation**: loss of energy with distance, measured in dB; remedy: amplifiers or repeaters. **Distortion**: change of shape, because frequency components travel at different speeds and arrive at different times (delay distortion); remedy: equalisation, shorter spans, single-mode fibre. **Noise**: unwanted signal added in transit (thermal, induced, crosstalk, impulse); remedy: shielding, twisting, higher SNR. Include one dB example: half the power is −3 dB.

</Deeper>
