## Short answers

<Predict
  id="q1-diff-switching"
  prompt="Differentiate between circuit switching and packet switching. (4 marks)"
  answer="Four contrasts: reserved vs on-demand resources, setup vs none, guaranteed vs variable rate/delay, idle capacity wasted vs shared (statistical multiplexing). Add an example of each."
>

| | Circuit switching | Packet switching |
|---|---|---|
| Resources | Reserved end to end for the call (dedicated, no sharing) | Used on demand; packets share links |
| Setup | Call setup needed before data flows | No call setup |
| Performance | Guaranteed, constant rate and delay | Variable delay; loss possible under congestion |
| Idle capacity | Wasted when the caller is silent | Used by other users (statistical multiplexing) |
| Example | Traditional telephone network | The Internet |

One-line closer for full marks: "With a 1 Mbps link and users needing 100 kbps active 10% of the time, circuit switching supports 10 users; packet switching supports 35 with under 0.0004 probability of more than 10 active."

</Predict>

<Predict
  id="q1-nodal-delay"
  prompt="Name the four sources of packet delay at a node and write the nodal delay formula. (4 marks)"
  answer="dnodal = dproc + dqueue + dtrans + dprop. Processing (check bit errors, pick output link), queueing (wait at the output buffer, depends on congestion), transmission L/R, propagation d/s."
>

**d_nodal = d_proc + d_queue + d_trans + d_prop**

- **Processing:** examine the header, check bit errors, decide the output link. Microseconds.
- **Queueing:** time waiting at the output link for earlier packets to be sent. Depends on congestion; zero to milliseconds.
- **Transmission:** L/R, the time to push all the packet's bits onto the link. Depends on packet length and link rate.
- **Propagation:** d/s, the time for one bit to travel the link. Depends on distance and the medium's speed (about 2×10⁸ m/s).

Graders look for the distinction between transmission and propagation: one depends on L and R, the other on d and s.

</Predict>

<Predict
  id="q1-why-layering"
  prompt="Why do we use a layered architecture? Give two advantages and one criticism. (3 marks)"
  answer="Explicit structure to identify the pieces and their relationships; modularization eases maintenance because a layer's implementation can change transparently. Criticism: duplication of functionality and layers needing information from other layers."
>

1. **Explicit structure:** a layered reference model lets you identify the pieces of a complex system and how they relate (the airline analogy: ticket, baggage, gates, runway, routing).
2. **Modularization:** changing how one layer implements its service is transparent to the rest of the system. A change in the gate procedure does not affect the rest of the trip; swapping Wi-Fi for Ethernet does not change HTTP.

**Criticism ("layering considered harmful?"):** layers can duplicate functions (error control at both link and transport), and a layer may need information that only another layer has, which strict layering forbids.

</Predict>

<Predict
  id="q1-transport-duties"
  prompt="According to the lecture slides, list the responsibilities of the transport layer. (4 marks)"
  answer="Process-to-process delivery of the message, via: service-point (port) addressing, segmentation and reassembly (sequence numbers), connection control (connectionless or connection-oriented), end-to-end flow control, process-to-process error control."
>

The transport layer delivers a message from one **process** to another. It is concerned with:

- **Service-point addressing:** port numbers pick the process.
- **Segmentation and reassembly:** the message is split into segments carrying sequence numbers, and put back in order at the receiver.
- **Connection control:** connectionless (UDP) or connection-oriented (TCP).
- **Flow control:** end to end. The slide's wording is "mechanism for overwhelming the receiver"; the intended meaning is a mechanism *to prevent* overwhelming the receiver. Write it that way.
- **Error control:** process to process, not per link.

</Predict>
